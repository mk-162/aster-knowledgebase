# Meet-ups

A meet-up is a ride or run your club gets together for — usually just for
your club's members, though you can open one to anyone on Aster instead. It
lives on the club page's **Rides** tab (alongside **Events**), members RSVP
with one tap, and a members-only meet-up gets a live map only members can
see.

The club page also leads with a **next-meet-up hero** on its home view:
whenever the club has an upcoming meet-up, the soonest one shows first, with
one-tap RSVP, so a member's first screen is the thing that matters most.

A meet-up's own page is the **same map-first page every Aster event uses** —
the live map is front and centre, and About, Chat and the post-ride Recap
live behind an **Info** sheet you open from the map. There's no separate
"club ride" page design any more; a meet-up is a first-class event that
happens to be run by a club.

## Create a meet-up

Club admins create meet-ups on a **dedicated "New meet-up" page** of its own
(not an inline form on the club diary):

1. Open your club's page and the **Rides** tab, then tap **New meet-up**.
2. Fill in the form:
   - **Activity name** and the **Ride/Run** toggle (a running club can
     schedule a run).
   - **Date & time** — can't be set in the past; the picker shows the
     timezone it's using underneath.
   - **Repeat weekly** (optional) — see below.
   - **Meeting point** (optional) — where the group rolls out from.
   - **Pace groups** (optional) — type a name and press Enter (or comma) to
     add it as a chip, e.g. "Fast", "Steady", "Social". These become groups
     on the meet-up, shown to everyone going.
   - **Route** (optional) — three ways to attach one: pick a **saved** route
     (your [club's library](the-club-route-library.md) plus your own personal
     **My routes**), **upload a GPX**, or **build** a brand-new route in the
     in-app route builder on the spot. See
     [the club route library](the-club-route-library.md) for the full detail.
   - **Who can see this** (optional, defaults to **just your club**) — a chip
     that opens a sheet with two choices: **just your club** (members-only —
     never appears in Discover, and only signed-in members can open it) or
     **anyone on Aster** (appears in Meet-ups discovery and anyone can find
     and join). Most club meet-ups are members-only; see
     [club visibility and sharing](club-visibility-and-sharing.md) for how a
     public one looks to non-members.
   - **Joining instructions** (optional, behind "+ Add details") — how the
     ride works: pace, stops, what to bring.
3. Tap **Create**.

If you leave the page with anything typed, Aster asks you to confirm before
discarding it — including if you step away to build a route and come back
(your form is restored exactly as you left it).

### After you create it

A plain meet-up (no competitive format — see below) shows a **"Meet-up
created"** confirmation with the essentials at a glance (name, time, audience,
whether a route attached) and:

- **Share link** — the one clear primary action, so you can get the meet-up
  in front of people straight away.
- **Send notification** — pushes an in-app notification (and a push
  notification, for anyone with the app) to every club member.
- **View meet-up** / **Edit meet-up** (opens [Manage meet-up](#manage-a-meetup))
  / **Create another meet-up** (resets the form).
- **Back to {club}** to finish up.

### Beyond a simple meet-up: club races and time trials {#beyond-a-simple-meetup-club-races-and-time-trials}

The New meet-up page defaults to an untimed **meet-up** format, but the
**Format** picker lets an admin **add a competitive format** instead — race,
time trial, sportive or point-to-point — all still free for a club to run,
members-only or public. Choosing one of these turns "Create" into the start
of full event setup: Aster takes you straight to that event's **Essentials**
screen to configure gates, registration and the rest, exactly like any
organiser-created event. The **Repeat weekly** toggle only applies to the
plain meet-up format — a race or TT is always one-off.

### Manage a meet-up {#manage-a-meetup}

Any event manager (the creator or a club admin) can tap **Edit** from the
meet-up's page at any time, including after it's rolled or gone live. This
opens **Manage meet-up**, the same console every organiser uses, with the
route, meeting point, audience and (once a route is attached) **Milestones**
all listed for editing.

## Milestones on a meet-up route

Once a meet-up has a route, admins can add **Milestones** — named landmarks
along it — from Manage meet-up. Members see them listed on the meet-up page
in advance and as markers on the map during the ride or run. Milestones are
purely informational until the meet-up is over — see
[the Club Recap](#the-club-recap) for what happens with them afterwards.

## Weekly repeating meet-ups

Switch on **Repeat weekly** and Aster infers the day from the date you
picked (a Wednesday evening ride defaults to repeating on Wednesdays) —
tap **Change** if you want a different day or an end date. Aster then
schedules the meet-up for you: the **next week's occurrence appears
automatically about seven days in advance**, so there's always exactly one
upcoming occurrence for members to RSVP to. Times follow your club's local
clock, so the 6:30pm chain-gang stays at 6:30pm across daylight-saving
changes.

Weekly meet-ups keep producing on every club — they're free to run, so
nothing ever stops the next occurrence appearing, and nothing is deleted. See
[club membership and Aster Plus](billing-and-your-club-plan.md).

## "We're rolling"

Once you're within the window before a meet-up starts (from 12 hours before
a timed meet-up, or any time for one with "Time TBC"), an event manager can
tap **We're rolling** to confirm the group has actually set off. This:

- Notifies the club that the group is rolling.
- Shows a calm banner on the meet-up page for everyone.
- **Freezes the Milestones list** for the ride.

**It never starts or stops anyone's tracking** — each athlete still taps
Start themselves on the Go screen. It's a human confirmation for the group,
not a control over anyone's ride. It can only be confirmed once per meet-up.

## RSVP

Members RSVP with one tap — **I'm going** from the Rides tab, or the same
RSVP control on the meet-up's own page. The RSVP count shows on the meet-up
card, and RSVPing is the same as joining any Aster event: when the meet-up
starts, you track it like any other ride or run. RSVP'd members who choose
not to track still count as attendees. Changed your mind? **Can't make it**
withdraws your RSVP. RSVP needs a connection (it's a registration, like
joining any event); tracking itself never does.

When a new meet-up is added, active members get a notification so it
doesn't slip by. Aster also sends **reminders** to members who've RSVP'd as
the meet-up approaches — one about a day before ("Ride tomorrow") and one
about an hour before ("Ride starting soon") — so nobody forgets they're in.

**Who's going** — anyone who can see the meet-up (a manager, a fellow
member, or anyone who's RSVP'd) sees the list of who's going as an
overlapping-avatar row with a "see all" expand. This is a lighter list than
[the register](#the-register) below, which is manager-only.

## The register {#the-register}

The RSVP list doubles as the meet-up's **attendance register** — many clubs
need one for their insurance. The meet-up's creator (and club admins) see, on
the meet-up page:

- every RSVP'd participant, and
- whether each of them actually tracked the ride or run,

with an **Export CSV** button that downloads the register as a spreadsheet
file — handy for club records or an insurer.

## "Everyone home?" {#everyone-home}

On the meet-up's **live map** (managers only), an **Everyone home?** panel
gives a glanceable list of where everyone stands:

- **Home** — finished their ride or run.
- **On the road** — tracking and recently updated.
- **Quiet Xm** — tracking, but no update for a while (often just a signal
  dead zone).
- **Not started** — RSVP'd but never set off (or their first ping hasn't
  synced yet).
- **Not taking part** — marked as not going, or didn't finish (a rare
  outcome on a meet-up — see [the Club Recap](#the-club-recap)).

**Important:** this panel is informational only. It never ends anyone's
activity, and neither can you from here — each person always ends their own
ride or run themselves. "Quiet" means "no recent update", not "stopped":
athletes in dead zones keep recording on their phone and their trail fills in
when signal returns. See
[offline and no signal](../safety-and-offline/offline-and-no-signal.md).

## The Club Recap {#the-club-recap}

Once a meet-up is under way or done, its **Recap** (in the Info sheet, or its
own tab on the meet-up page) turns from "check back after the ride" into the
factual story of what happened — members-only, and deliberately **not
raced**:

- **Milestone arrival order**, if the route had any — who reached each one
  and in what order, with a gap to the rider ahead. Two riders too close to
  call apart share a placement (shown as **"=2nd"**, for example) rather than
  being forced into a false order, and Aster says so plainly when GPS was too
  coarse to be sure.
- **Who came along** — attendance, with distance and moving time for anyone
  who tracked.
- **Watch the replay** — a link into the existing multi-rider replay of the
  ride or run.
- If you've done this meet-up before (a weekly series), a private comparison
  against your own past attempt — visible only to you.

Ending a meet-up normally — reaching the end, or just stopping tracking —
marks an athlete as **Finished**, not DNF. A meet-up has no "race result": it
is, in the app's own words, "a shared ride on open roads, not a race." DNF is
only possible on a meet-up that was given a competitive format (see
[above](#beyond-a-simple-meetup-club-races-and-time-trials)) and someone
formally abandons it.

## Meet-ups are free on every club

Every club gets the full meet-up toolkit for nothing — one-off **and**
weekly repeating meet-ups, the route builder, RSVP, register, CSV export,
Milestones, the Club Recap, "We're rolling", pace groups. None of it is
paywalled, and the club never pays a subscription. See
[club membership and Aster Plus](billing-and-your-club-plan.md) for how
members join.
