# Handling alerts

An athlete in your event can raise an **Alert** if they need help. It reaches
**you, the organisers**, so your dashboard is where you see and manage it.

## What an Alert is, and is not

Be clear on this before your event, and brief your athletes on it too (see
[What to tell your riders before the event](brief-your-riders.md)).

- An Alert is an **in-app notification and location share**. It tells the
  organisers that an athlete needs help and shows their last known position.
  It's a call for attention, not a guaranteed emergency response.
- Aster **does not contact the emergency services**. It cannot call for an
  athlete. The athlete's own screen offers a one-tap **Call emergency
  services** button (it dials 112, which reaches the emergency services
  across the UK and the EU), but placing that call is down to the athlete or
  to you. The app never does it.
- An Alert **does not guarantee anyone will respond**. It's an aid, not a
  rescue service. Your event still needs a real emergency plan.

Deciding whether and when to escalate to the emergency services is **your
call**, not the app's.

## Good to know: the alert doesn't fire instantly

When an athlete raises an Alert, they get a 60-second countdown with a large
**Cancel** button, so an accidental tap doesn't send a false alert to your
dashboard. If they don't cancel, the Alert sends and you're notified. They
can also mark it a **false alarm** themselves from their own screen after
sending, which closes it the same way your False alarm button does.

## What you see

Active Alerts appear on your [race-day dashboard](race-day-management.md),
highlighted so they're impossible to miss, and refreshed every few seconds.
Each one shows:

- The **athlete** who raised it.
- Their **last known location**, so you can find them.
- When it was raised and its current **status**.

If the athlete has chosen to share their phone number for emergencies, it
appears on the alert too. Otherwise it stays hidden.

Resolved and past alerts move to a separate **Alert history** view, so the
live dashboard only shows what's still open.

## Responding

You move an alert through these states:

- **Responding**: you've seen it and you're dealing with it. This records
  that you're on it and who acknowledged it.
- **Resolved**: the situation is handled. This closes the alert.
- **False alarm**: not a real emergency (an accidental trigger, say). This
  also closes the alert.

If nobody marks an alert **Responding** within a couple of minutes, Aster
notifies the organisers again, so an open alert can't quietly sit unseen.

You can **message the athlete** directly from the alert. They receive your
message as a notification and can reply, giving you a two-way thread while
you coordinate help.

## Important: an Alert never ends the ride

Raising an Alert is completely separate from finishing. The athlete keeps
being tracked, which is exactly what you want when someone may need help, so
their live location stays visible and keeps updating. To end an athlete's
ride you set their status yourself (see
[Running the event on race day](race-day-management.md)). An Alert on its
own changes nothing about their race record.

## Good to know: automatic crash detection covers your event too

Aster can automatically start an alert countdown if it detects a hard,
sudden stop, whether the athlete is on a **[Just Ride](../for-athletes/free-rides.md)**
or tracking your event, whatever the format: race, time trial, sportive,
meet-up or point-to-point. The countdown and the alert it raises are
identical either way, so everything above (the 60-second cancellable
countdown, what appears on your dashboard, how you respond) applies the
same to a crash-triggered alert as to one an athlete raises by hand. There
is no separate badge or flag to tell the two apart on your side.
